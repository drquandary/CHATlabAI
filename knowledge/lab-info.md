# Penn Center for Neuroaesthetics (PCfN) — Lab Info

> Context grounding for CHATLabAI. Keeps literature reviews and writing aligned with the lab's frame.

## Mission

The Penn Center for Neuroaesthetics investigates the neural and cognitive foundations of
aesthetic experience — how and why humans find things beautiful, moving, or meaningful.
Directed by Anjan Chatterjee. The lab bridges cognitive neuroscience, neurology, and the
humanities (art, architecture, design).

## The Aesthetic Triad

Chatterjee's framework: aesthetic experience emerges from three interacting neural systems:

1. **Sensorimotor systems** — perception of objects/scenes (visual form, motion, spatial
   organization) and the body's sensorimotor engagement with them.
2. **Emotion-valuation systems** — the affective and reward responses that make something
   appealing, beautiful, or desirable (pleasure, liking, wanting).
3. **Meaning-knowledge systems** — top-down, culture- and experience-laden interpretation
   that shapes what something means and why it matters (semantic, contextual, narrative).

These are distributed, overlapping, and not strictly modular. Claims about aesthetics should
reference the relevant system(s) and remain mechanism-cautious.

## Methods

- **fMRI** — brain mapping of aesthetic judgment, face/scene/art perception.
- **Lesion / neuropsychology** — patients with focal lesions informing the neural basis of aesthetics.
- **Behavioral empirical aesthetics** — reaction-time, rating, preference, and forced-choice paradigms.

## Domain vocabulary

- **Beauty** — the canonical aesthetic positive; studied via face, scene, object, and art beauty.
- **Faces** — attractiveness, prototype, symmetry, gaze.
- **Art** — representational/abstract; style, composition, context.
- **Architecture & design perception** — built form, spatial aesthetics, wayfinding, monumentality.
- **Empirical aesthetics** — the methodological frame for studying aesthetic experience experimentally.

## Convergence principle (ties to writing rule 5)

The lab values convergence across method types (behavioral + lesion + imaging) and across
stimulus domains (face + scene + art + architecture) over any single striking effect.
